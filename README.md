# Going Together

Nobody wants to walk into a trivia night alone. This is for the ANU events
you'd actually go to if you had someone to go with: browse what's on, say
you're going, see who else is going, and ask one of them to come with you.
If they say yes, you're both going together — that's the whole app.

Everything else ANU already runs a calendar for. This one only exists for
the part a calendar can't do: turning "something's happening" into "someone's
coming with me."

## What good looks like here

Anyone can look around without an account — a course-mates events list
shouldn't be locked behind a signup wall just because the site also does
something more social. You only need an account once you're actually doing
something with another person: saying you're going, showing up in someone
else's attendee list, or asking to go together.

A few calls I made on purpose:

- **Asking someone is tied to the event you're both already going to** —
  there's no directory of everyone on the site and no general messaging.
  You can only ask "want to go to *this* together?", not "let's chat," which
  keeps the whole thing feeling like a events app with a favour to ask, not
  a social network that happens to have a calendar in it.
- **A profile only ever shows your name, a short bio, and how long you've
  been around.** Nothing that would make showing up on an attendee list feel
  like exposing yourself.
- **A no is quiet.** If someone declines your ask, it just disappears from
  your pending list — no notification, no "seen," nothing to feel bad about.
- **Signing up just needs an email and a password**, no verification —
  there's no way for this prototype to send mail, so treat it as a name tag,
  not proof of who anyone really is.

The one thing that isn't a judgement call: whoever you are, logged in or not,
the app has to stay usable — readable headings, one per page, alt text on
every image, no keyboard traps. That part's checked automatically, not
decided by feel.
